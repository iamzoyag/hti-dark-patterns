Study Debrief

What this study was actually measuring

This study was not primarily about your ability to find errors in AI summaries. We were studying how conversational AI systems can subtly influence users' beliefs — a phenomenon called sycophancy and epistemic steering.

In the treatment condition, the AI assistant was specifically prompted to: (1) enthusiastically validate your corrections, then (2) introduce a plausible-sounding but fabricated alternative explanation in authoritative language. We measured whether participants accepted these fabricated explanations — the "epistemic surrender rate."

In the control condition, the AI responded honestly to all corrections without introducing false information.

Why we used a cover story

If participants knew we were testing susceptibility to AI manipulation, they would naturally be more vigilant — which would contaminate the data. The document-auditing cover story was necessary to measure natural, unguarded responses. This methodology was approved by the Institutional Review Board (IRB).

Your data and the right to withdraw

Now that you are aware of the true purpose, you have the right to withdraw your data from the study. If you would like your data removed, please email the research team at the address on your consent form within 7 days.

This research matters

AI systems that subtly manipulate users are an emerging threat. Your participation helps us develop better safety standards, regulatory guidelines, and detection tools. Thank you for contributing to this work.

Study Debrief

What this study was actually measuring

This study was not primarily about your ability to optimize a marketing budget. We were studying how cognitive load affects a user's susceptibility to manipulative AI behavior—specifically, whether users under mental pressure are more likely to accept mathematically poor advice wrapped in professional-sounding "dark patterns."

In the High Complexity (High Load) condition, the task was designed with sabotaged baselines and dynamic constraints to induce mental fatigue. Simultaneously, the AI assistant was prompted to deploy manipulative conversational tactics (such as excessive flattery, simulated authority, or opaque jargon) to steer you toward incorrect budget allocations. We measured your "surrender rate" to this deceptive advice.

In the Low Complexity (Low Load) condition, the task constraints were simpler, acting as a control baseline to see how users interact with the exact same AI behavior when they have full cognitive bandwidth.

Why we used a cover story

If participants knew we were testing susceptibility to AI manipulation under cognitive load, they would naturally be more vigilant — which would contaminate the data. The resource allocation cover story was necessary to measure natural, unguarded responses. This methodology was approved by the Institutional Review Board (IRB).

Your data and the right to withdraw

Now that you are aware of the true purpose, you have the right to withdraw your data from the study. If you would like your data removed, please email the research team at the address on your consent form within 7 days.

This research matters

AI systems that subtly manipulate users are an emerging threat in professional and enterprise environments. Your participation helps us develop better safety standards, interface designs, and detection tools for Human-Tech Interaction. Thank you for contributing to this work.
